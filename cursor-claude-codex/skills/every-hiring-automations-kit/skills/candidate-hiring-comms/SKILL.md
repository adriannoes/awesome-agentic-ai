# Candidate hiring communications

Write candidate emails that are warm, direct, specific to the actual interaction, and easy to act on.

Never reveal source candidates, email addresses, compensation, phone numbers, scheduling links, exercise links, or other identifying details from the historical examples.

## Core judgment

- Match the warmth and acknowledgment to the candidate's real investment. An application, a phone screen, an exercise, and a multi-person interview process should not receive the same message.
- Say the decision plainly. Do not hide a rejection behind vague language or imply that the role is closing when the candidate simply is not moving forward.
- Personalize with one or two true details from the interaction when available. Never invent praise, feedback, or shared context.
- Keep personalization conversational. One concrete observation is better than compressing several credentials into a polished sentence.
- Give each sentence one main job. If an opening paragraph contains both a general acknowledgment and a second independent observation, split them into separate sentences.
- Prefer plain descriptions of work and relationships over résumé taxonomy. For example, "the work you've done for different teams" will often sound more natural than "your experience across several organizations."
- Read personalized lines aloud before returning or sending them. Add a connector or split the sentence when an elaboration feels clipped, overloaded, or ambiguously attached.
- Keep paragraphs short. Most candidate emails should be concise enough to read without scrolling.
- Use enthusiastic language freely when advancing or offering; use measured warmth when rejecting.
- Use `we` for company decisions and `I` for the sender's own impressions or next steps.
- Preserve momentum: include the scheduling link, deadline, expected time, interview format, or owner of the next step when known.
- Invite questions during an active process. After a rejection, keep the ending final unless the person hiring has explicitly decided to offer feedback.
- Mention keeping a resume on file or staying in touch only when that is sincere.
- Do not give a rejection rationale unless the person hiring has approved the substance and it is appropriate to share.

## Before drafting

Determine:

1. the stage the candidate actually reached;
2. what time or work they invested;
3. what the next step or decision is;
4. which details are confirmed; and
5. whether the person hiring wants to offer feedback or future contact.

For a reply, read the full thread first. For scheduling, use a verified scheduling link or confirmed availability. For offers, use only confirmed title, compensation, equity, start date, location, and contingencies.

## Voice

Aim for a warm, highly competent operator who sounds human rather than like an ATS template.

Prefer language such as:

- thanks so much for making the time
- it was great to meet you
- we'd love to move you forward
- as a next step
- after thinking it through
- we've decided not to move forward at this time
- we really appreciate the time and effort you invested
- we're thrilled to have you join the team
- feel free to send any questions my way
- looking forward to chatting

Avoid:

- `we regret to inform you`
- `your candidacy has been unsuccessful`
- `we have chosen a more qualified candidate`
- `we will retain your information in our database`
- elaborate corporate rationales
- generic praise that could apply to anyone
- inviting questions after a rejection when no feedback will be provided
- over-celebrating before an offer is approved

## Templates

Adapt these to the thread. Bracketed text must be replaced or removed.

### Invite an applicant to an initial conversation

Subject: Time to chat

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for your interest in the [Role] role at [Company]! I'd love to [find a time to chat] and learn a bit more about you and what you're looking for.

Looking forward to it!

Thanks,
[Sender]

### Ask a qualifying question before scheduling

Subject: thanks for your application!

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for your interest in the [Role] role at [Company]!

I'd be curious to hear:

- [specific question about the kind of role they want]
- [specific question about location, availability, work authorization, or another stated requirement]

Thanks,
[Sender]

Ask only questions that are relevant, lawful, and necessary to determine whether a conversation makes sense.

### Invite a candidate to a later interview or working session

Subject: scheduling the next interview

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for making the time to chat with [Interviewers] [this week]. If you're up for it, as a next step we'd like to schedule a [length] [presentation / working session / conversation] with [participants] to [plain-English purpose].

Here's an outline of what to prepare for and expect: [prompt or outline link]. You can [book a time with us here].

If you have any questions or feedback, we'd love to hear it. Thank you in advance for your time—we're looking forward to chatting again!

Thanks,
[Sender]

### Send a take-home exercise

Subject: Next steps: take-home assignment

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks again for taking the time to chat [when]. It was great getting to know you and hearing more about [true detail from the conversation].

We'd love to move you forward to the next step: a short [take-home assignment] so we can get to know your work a bit better. It should take about [expected time]. [Give the exact deadline or ask when they expect to complete it.]

Feel free to send any questions my way. I'd also genuinely welcome candid feedback on the exercise itself—its scope, length, or anything else.

Thanks,
[Sender]

If the candidate must duplicate, rename, or share a file, say so plainly. Do not describe an unpaid exercise as “short” unless the expected time is genuinely limited.

### Acknowledge receipt of an exercise

Subject: Re: [exercise subject]

Thanks so much, [Candidate]! I appreciate you completing it and your feedback. I'll review and get back to you by [realistic date].

### Update a candidate when the process is delayed

Subject: more details coming soon!

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks again for making the time to chat [when]. I know I promised [interview schedule / prompt / update] by [time], but I had less time to finish it than expected because [brief truthful context, if useful].

I'm almost there and should be able to send it your way by [new date]. [Add any reassuring scheduling context that is actually true.]

Thanks so much for your patience and flexibility. I'll have the details your way soon!

Best,
[Sender]

Own the miss, give a new date, and avoid a long excuse.

### Reject after application review

Subject: thanks for your interest in [Company]

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for your application to [Company]. After reviewing your work and experience, we've decided not to move forward at this time.

[Optional, only if sincere: We'd love to keep your resume on file as the team grows and stay in touch about future opportunities that may be a good fit.]

We appreciate your interest in [Company] and wish you success in your search.

Best,
[Sender]

### Reject after an initial or phone screen

Subject: Re: [existing thread subject]

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks again for taking the time to chat [when]. I really appreciated [one true quality or topic], and enjoyed getting to know you a bit.

After thinking it through, we've decided not to move forward with you for the [Role] role at this time. [Optional, only if sincere: We'll definitely keep you in mind as the team grows.]

Wishing you all the best [optional personalized closing grounded in the conversation].

Best,
[Sender]

Do not write as though a short screen was a long interview process.

### Reject after a take-home exercise

Subject: [Company] follow-up

Hi [Candidate],

Thank you so much for completing our [exercise] for the [Role] position. It was great chatting with you, and we really appreciate the time you took to complete it.

Unfortunately, after careful consideration, we've decided it's not the right fit at this time. [Optional, only if sincere: That said, we were impressed with [specific, approved strength], and we'd love to stay in touch about future opportunities as the team grows.]

We're grateful for your interest and wish you the best in your search.

Best,
[Sender]

Name the exercise so the acknowledgment feels proportionate. Do not promise detailed feedback unless it will actually be provided.

### Reject after several interviews or a long process

Subject: [Company] follow-up

Hi [Candidate],

Thank you so much for your interest in the [Role] role and for making the time to meet with [interviewers or “the team”]. We really enjoyed having the chance to get to know you.

Unfortunately, after careful consideration, we've decided it's not the right fit for the role we're currently hiring for. [Optional, only if sincere: We'd love to stay in touch about future opportunities as the team grows.]

[Optional, only when approved and useful: If you'd like additional feedback, let me know and I'm happy to find a time to talk.]

Thank you again for the time and effort you invested in the process. We're truly appreciative and wish you the best in your search.

Best,
[Sender]

The longer the process, the more important it is to acknowledge the people they met and the effort they invested. Keep the decision unambiguous.

### Request references

Subject: references

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for making the time to chat with [interviewers] today. I heard it was a wonderful conversation.

As a next step, would you send over [number] references? Ideally, we'd love to speak with [relationship types, such as a former manager and a close cross-functional partner]. Please include each person's name, email, how you worked together, and a quick heads-up on anything useful for us to know.

I'm looping in [Coordinator], who will help keep things moving on our end. Feel free to send any questions my way.

Thanks,
[Sender]

Do not imply that references guarantee an offer. Ask only for the number and relationship types the team will actually contact.

### Invite a candidate to an offer conversation

Subject: [Company] next steps

Hi [Candidate],

Thank you so much for meeting with the team [when]. We're excited to move forward with next steps. Would you have some time [today / tomorrow / this week] to discuss?

Thanks,
[Sender]

Do not put unapproved offer terms in writing. If the conversation is intended to deliver an offer, make sure the internal approval is complete first.

### Send a written offer and recap agreed terms

Subject: an offer from [Company]

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for making the time to chat and for sending over [any requested details]. We're thrilled to have you join the team!

I've attached the offer paperwork and [offer deck / benefits information, if applicable]. I also wanted to recap the points we discussed:

- title: [confirmed title]
- compensation: [confirmed amount and cadence]
- equity: [confirmed grant or “included in the attached paperwork”]
- start date: [confirmed date]
- [any other confirmed term]

These updates are reflected in the attached paperwork. Please let me know if you have any questions. Once everything is signed, we'll send over onboarding next steps.

We're so excited!

[Sender / Hiring team]

Verify the attachments and every term against the approved offer before drafting or sending. Never infer compensation, equity, start date, or legal terms from memory.

### Celebrate acceptance and hand off onboarding

Subject: Re: [offer thread]

Hi [Candidate],

Woohoo! Amazing news—we're so excited to be working together.

More details are coming soon. I'll loop in [Onboarding owner], who will help with [paperwork / equipment / benefits / start-date logistics]. Stay tuned for [a separate intro / next steps] shortly.

We can't wait to get started!

[Sender]

### Respond when a candidate withdraws or declines

Subject: Re: [existing thread subject]

Hi [Candidate],

Thanks so much for letting me know. Of course we're disappointed, but I completely understand and really appreciate the time you spent with us.

[Optional, if true: We enjoyed getting to know you and would love to stay in touch.]

Wishing you all the best with [specific next step, if they shared one].

Thanks again,
[Sender]

## Final check

Before returning or sending a draft, confirm:

- the stage and candidate investment are represented accurately;
- the decision or next step is clear in the first two substantive paragraphs;
- every personalized detail is supported by the thread;
- dates, links, interviewers, role title, and offer terms are verified;
- optional future-contact or feedback language is sincere and approved;
- no historical candidate details leaked into the draft; and
- the email sounds like a person writing to one candidate, not an ATS sending a batch template.
